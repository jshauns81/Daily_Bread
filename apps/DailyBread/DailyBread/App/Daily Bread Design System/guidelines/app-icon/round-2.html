Round two — does it say Daily Bread?

You're right and I was wrong. An icon's first job is identification, and I skipped straight to systemic elegance. Five beautiful abstractions that leave you squinting at your own dock is a failure, however well the grid ties to the app.

Worse, I talked myself out of the bread on grounds that don't hold. Bread is the strongest asset the brand has. It's the name; it's the family table; and "bread" already means money — which is the app's one unbreakable colour invariant. That's not a cute pun, it's the whole product in one word. Below, the same ladder and a dock test.

The directions

F · The loafMy pick

A domed loaf with three scores, gold on cream. Unmistakable in a dock at a glance — which was your entire point — and warm rather than cute, because it's a confident geometric silhouette and not a character with a face.

The scores are what keep it from reading as a generic blob at small sizes; they give it a texture that survives down to 40pt.

G · The sliceMost distinctive silhouette

One slice, wider than it is tall — that proportion is what separates it from a door or an arch, and it's the thing I got wrong twice. Crust rim inside a continuous outline; the shoulders sit at the top of the body and the crown rises from within them, never overhanging.

More graphic than a whole loaf, and the flat base gives it a steadier footing at small sizes. The crumb area also leaves room for a gold cell or the rainbow later without redrawing the mark.

H · Loaf with a rainbow crustBridges both rounds

The loaf silhouette, filled with the year's spectrum. Says "Daily Bread" instantly and carries the rainbow year that runs through the app. The one option that answers your question without giving up round one.

Risk: two ideas in one mark. At 29pt the spectrum collapses to a warm blur — which is fine, because the silhouette is still doing the identifying.

I · WheatMost timeless, least literal

A single stalk. Older and quieter than a loaf, with a heraldic feel that won't date — and it carries harvest and earning without depicting a food item.

Costs: the thinnest forms here, so it's the most fragile at 29pt, and "wheat" reads as bakery or farm before it reads as family.

The dock test

Your actual scenario. Glance down, don't study. The icon you ship today is first, so baseline → alternatives reads left to right. F and G name themselves immediately; H does too, with more life in it; I needs a beat. Round one's grid is last — notice it's still the prettiest tile and still the one you'd have to explain.

Where I'd land

F or H. Take F if you want the mark to be quiet and certain; take H if you want the app's personality on the home screen. G is the safest bet if it's ever going to sit next to other apps' icons at tiny sizes.

And a note on execution, since it decides whether this works: the difference between a warm loaf and a cute loaf is entirely in the drawing. No face, no steam lines, no sparkles. One weight of form, generous curves, a slightly matte gold — closer to a woodcut or a stamp than to an illustration. That's the instruction to give whoever redraws it.

These are CSS silhouette studies, so the curves are approximate — a real vector pass will do the loaf far more justice than I can here.
