Round four — it was never a loaf

That reframes the whole thing, and it makes the icon easier. The Eucharistic bread is a round host — and a round gold disc is also a coin. Daily bread and daily money are the same shape. You don't need a metaphor that carries both meanings; the form already does.

A circle is also the single most legible form at 29pt, which quietly solves the problem that killed the 7×7 grid. Three rounds of loaf-drawing were chasing the wrong noun.

How overt do you want it?

This is the question I can't answer for you, and it should be settled before anything is drawn properly. The same form can sit anywhere on this scale:

QuietAn unmarked disc.
Reads as coin, bread, sun, cell.
ImpliedParallel incisions.
Bread scoring, no symbol.
ExplicitAn incised cross.
Unmistakably Eucharistic.
My instinct is Implied — three parallel slashes are how bread is genuinely scored before baking, so the mark stays bread and never becomes a symbol the app has to explain. Explicit keeps the cross but cuts it as a real incision rather than an applied bar; note that even done well it dominates the tile, because a centred equal-armed cross always will. Your family's app and your metaphor — this one is genuinely yours to call.

The directions

N · The scored hostMy pick

A gold disc with three shallow incisions, the way bread is genuinely scored before baking. Reads as bread to anyone who bakes, as a coin to a child counting earnings, and — with the cross variant above — as a host to you. Nothing is announced; everything is present.

It's also the most legible thing in any of the four rounds — a filled circle survives every size, and the scoring is a texture rather than a detail, so it doesn't vanish at 29pt.

O · BrokenMost meaningful

The fraction — bread broken and shared. Two halves, slightly parted. For an app about a family dividing work and provision between them, this is the closest any mark here gets to saying what Daily Bread actually is.

It's the most emotionally loaded option and the most fragile: the gap must stay wide enough to read at 29pt without the halves drifting into two unrelated shapes.

P · The rainbow hostKeeps the grid

The disc, filled with the year's cells. Everything you didn't want to lose from round one, in a form that finally holds together small — the circle does the identifying, the cells do the meaning.

Compared with the mosaic loaf, this is cleaner: a circle tolerates a coarse grid far better than a loaf silhouette does, because there's no delicate outline for the cells to fight.

Q · Broken, with the year insideBoth ideas

O and P together — the fraction, with the year showing in the break. The most complete statement of the product, and the busiest mark of the four.

I'd only take this if it survives a vector pass at 40pt. It's carrying a lot.

Dock test

Current icon first, and the two best loaves from rounds two and three at the end. Note how much better every circle holds at 64px than either loaf does — that's not taste, it's the form. A disc has no thin features to lose.

Where I'd land

N if you want the app to be quiet about it, O if you want it to mean something out loud. P is the one to take if the grid still matters most to you — and it's a genuinely better home for the grid than the loaf was.

One production note, since gold is doing a lot of work here: make the disc matte, not metallic. A gradient with any shine reads as a coin-collecting game. A flat or barely-graded gold reads as bread, wheat and warmth — which is the register the rest of the app is in.

CSS studies. A circle is the one form I can draw accurately here, so these are closer to final than the loaves were — but the break edge in O and Q should be irregular in vector, not the clean straight cut I can manage in CSS.
