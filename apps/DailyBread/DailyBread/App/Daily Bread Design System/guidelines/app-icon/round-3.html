Round three — both, not either

You don't want to give up the grid, and you're right that it doesn't announce itself. Those aren't actually in conflict: the grid can be what the bread is made of. Every option below is one silhouette, filled with the year.

One thing that lowers the stakes first: the app's name is always rendered directly under the icon — home screen, Launchpad, Spotlight, Settings. The icon never has to carry identification alone. It has to be recognisable once you know it, and not look like a different product. That's a much lower bar than "explains itself to a stranger", and it's why abstract marks work fine for apps people actually use.

The synthesis

J · Mosaic loafMy pick

The loaf silhouette, built from the year's cells rather than filled with a gradient. At a glance it's bread; up close it's your heatmap. This is the only option here where the two ideas are the same object instead of one layered over the other.

It also degrades in the right direction — as it shrinks the cells fuse into a warm loaf, so it gets more readable, not less.

K · Crust and crumbCleanest structure

A gold crust holding a rainbow crumb. The bread reading comes entirely from the outline, so the interior is free to be the year — and the two never compete, because they're doing different jobs.

The most "designed" of the three, and the easiest to redraw well in vector. Also the easiest to animate later, if the crumb ever mirrors the real week.

L · Loaf with one gold cellMost restrained

A muted rainbow loaf with a single cell burning gold — today. It carries the grid, the bread and the money invariant in one mark, and it's the only one that changes meaning as the day goes on.

Quietest of the three. Risk: at 29pt the single cell is nearly lost, so it's really a large-size idea.

Dock test

Current icon first, then the three, then round two's plain loaf and round one's plain grid at the end. The question to ask isn't "what is that" — the name is under it — but "does that look like a real product, and would I recognise it tomorrow."

With the name attached

How it actually appears everywhere it matters. The word does the naming; the mark does the recognising.

Where I'd land

J. It's the one that doesn't compromise — you keep the grid entirely, and it reads as bread because the silhouette is doing that work independently. K is the safer commission if you're paying an illustrator, because the outline and the fill can be perfected separately.

And the pin you put in widgets matters here: once the widget carries the living rainbow, the icon is free to be a fixed mark. It doesn't have to show today's state, because the thing right next to it already does. That takes all the pressure off this decision.
