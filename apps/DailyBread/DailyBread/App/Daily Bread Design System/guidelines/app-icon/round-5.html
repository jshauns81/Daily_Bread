Round five — the monstrance

This is the best idea yet, and I don't think it was an accident. A radiant sunburst holding a disc, topped with a cross, is a monstrance — the vessel that displays the host. You've drawn the one object whose whole purpose is showing the bread.

It also carries dawn, which no other direction did: morning, a new day, give us this day. That's the app's actual rhythm.

So the concept is right. What follows is only about what has to be cut for it to survive being 29 pixels wide.

The problem, shown rather than argued

As referencedCollapses below 60pt

My approximation of your reference — thin rays, a detailed finial, three landscape planes with hairlines, and a soft bloom. Watch the ladder: it's handsome at 180 and by 40 it's an amber smudge on charcoal.

Nothing here is badly drawn. There's just more information than the format can carry.

What's spending the budget

  • ~14 rays, most under 1px at 60pt
  • An ornate finial — fleur tips resolve to mush
  • Three landscape planes plus hairline edges — a second scene competing with the first
  • A radial bloom, which turns to grey haze when downsampled
  • A baked-in bezel — iOS masks and shades the tile itself, so a drawn frame double-borders

What it can afford

  • 8 rays, thick, with real gaps
  • One silhouette — sun, or sun + horizon, not sun + hills + fields
  • A simple cross, or none
  • Flat colour, no glow
  • Full bleed to the tile edge, no frame
The rule of thumb: an app icon has roughly one shape and one modifier of capacity. Your reference has about five. Everything below keeps the idea and spends the budget once.

The reductions

V · Monstrance, reducedMy pick

Eight thick rays, a solid half-sun on the horizon, one small cross. Same reading as your reference at a glance — radiance, dawn, the host displayed — with a quarter of the parts.

Cream ground rather than charcoal, so it matches Sunroom and stops the gold from doing all the work alone.

W · Dawn, no crossThe quiet version

The same mark with the finial removed. Still a monstrance in structure, but it reads first as a sunrise — the meaning is available without being declared. This is the Implied position from round four, in your form rather than mine.

X · Full host, radiantMost legible

A whole disc rather than a half — the host itself, with rays behind it. Loses the dawn reading but gains a strong centre, which is what makes an icon hold at 29pt. Structurally the closest to a real monstrance.

Y · Reduced, on your dark groundKeeps your mood

V's geometry on the deep blue you chose. Warmer and more nocturnal — and a genuinely good argument for a dark tile, since gold on dark is how candlelight actually looks.

One caveat: it matches no shipping theme, which was strike two against the current icon. Worth doing anyway if the mood matters more, but it should be a decision rather than an inheritance.

Dock test

Current icon, your reference as-drawn, then the four reductions, with round four's scored host last. The reference and the current icon fail the same way — fine detail on dark, indistinguishable at a glance.

Where I'd land

V, or W if you want it quieter. You found the right object; it only needs to be drawn for the size it will actually live at. If you want the dark ground, Y is the same mark and I'd support it — just choose it deliberately.

Two notes for the vector pass: rays should be trapezoids, not rectangles — wider at the outer end, so they read as light rather than spokes. And no bezel or inner border; iOS masks the tile and adds its own shading, so a drawn frame ends up double-edged.

The top tile is my CSS approximation of your reference, not your actual file — close enough to demonstrate the size behaviour, not a substitute for testing the real artwork.
