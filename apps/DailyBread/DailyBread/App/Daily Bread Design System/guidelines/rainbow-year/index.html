The rainbow year

Your original idea, rebuilt — and the three decisions inside it that I can't make for you. Scroll the strip sideways; hover a day.

1 — The wheel doesn't restart, it continues

Day-of-year maps to hue, so the year is the colour wheel. The part that makes it yours: because day 365 lands at hue 359° and the next day 1 lands at 0°, the seam is invisible. Scrolling back through years is one unbroken rainbow — rainbow-start to rainbow-start, as you put it. Three years run continuously below, no gap between them; the ticks mark where each begins. It opens on the first boundary.

Why this beats a GitHub heatmap: a green-scale grid says only "how much". This says when — a March day is unmistakably a March colour, so a kid learns the shape of their own year. It also means a screenshot of any single week is uniquely identifiable, which is the sort of thing kids notice and like.

2 — Decided: optionals add bloom, never withhold colour

This is the hard part you flagged. Seven required, two open-ended (the "3× a week" kind) = 9 possible. He does 7. Is that day complete? Below is the same week under both rules — five of the seven days are ones where he met every requirement and left an optional untaken.

My strong recommendation is B. Under A, declining an optional he was never obliged to do leaves a visibly dimmer day on his year, forever — the calendar quietly punishes him for exercising a choice you deliberately gave him. That's the opposite of what an optional chore is. Under B, meeting the requirement is full brightness and optionals add a bloom on top: nothing is ever lost, only gained. This is the chosen behaviour.

3 — The five states

Each is the same hue at a different intensity, so the rainbow survives regardless of performance — a bad week is a desaturated week, not a grey hole.

On the glow. Perfect days get a real bloom — shadow at the cell's own hue, plus a lifted core. On a wall of 365 that reads as scattered embers, and a good streak becomes a visible burning line. That's the payoff for R11 living on the same surface.

4 — Decided: density

A full year at a readable cell size is wider than a phone. Three honest options — the strip above is Scroll.

✓ Decided — Card (last 12 weeks) is what sits on Today. Full year stays scrollable, one tap away.

5 — What I'd build

One RainbowYear view, three sizes. Card (last 12 weeks, on Today), Full (scrollable year, tap to open a day), Wall (iPad/macOS, whole year plus multi-year scroll). Same cell renderer, same maths, one component.

Cell payload. { date, requiredDone, requiredTotal, optionalDone, optionalTotal, isFuture } — hue from day-of-year, intensity from requiredDone/requiredTotal, bloom from optionalDone. The server already knows all five numbers.

Multi-year scroll loads a year at a time and keeps hue continuous across the boundary, so it genuinely never ends — which was the point.
