Second medium: Today, interactive

A separate widget kind, not a replacement. The year widget answers "how am I doing"; this one answers "what's left" and lets a kid check it off without opening the app (App Intents, iOS 17+). Offering both and letting them choose is cheaper than guessing.

Lock Screen & StandBy

Accessory widgets are monochrome — iOS tints them, so colour carries no meaning and the rainbow cannot appear. Everything reads from shape and fill alone. StandBy (landscape, charging, bedside) is the exception and gets 26 weeks at full colour.

Single-child rule holds here too. With one child, no name labels, no per-child rows, no picker — the parent's medium shows the chores, not "Ada: 3 of 6".

Implementation notes

① Interactive widgets via App Intents (iOS 17+). Checking a chore from the medium widget without opening the app is the highest-value item here — the core interaction, on the home screen. Button(intent: CompleteChoreIntent(id:)).

② Reload on completion, not just on a timer. WidgetCenter.shared.reloadTimelines(ofKind:) after every mutation, plus a morning refresh at the household's day-start hour.

③ The widget owns its theme. Read the active theme from the shared App Group container. A widget in default colours beside a Harbor app looks broken — and widgets run in a separate process, so this is easy to forget.

④ Never show money on the Lock Screen by default. It's visible to anyone who picks up the phone. Ship count-only; make the balance opt-in.

⑤ Snapshots must be instant. Cache last-known state in the App Group so getSnapshot never waits on the server — a spinning widget is worse than a stale one.

⑥ Hue is dayOfYear / 365 × 360, always — never position-within-window. Get this wrong and every widget renders a full spectrum regardless of range, which destroys the property that makes the year legible.

⑦ macOS: same widgets, plus the live dock icon (§1.2a) — the one surface where the mark itself can carry state.
